What a pipeline is evidence of

Every Monday somebody reads out a pipeline number and the room nods. Tonight asks what that number is evidence of. A pipeline is a list of claims about buyers: this one confirmed a need, that one let us run a trial, this one asked for a contract. When the stages record what the buyer did, the pipeline is evidence. When they record what the rep did, it is a diary. Part 1 · covers stages as buyer commitments

A stage is something the buyer did

5 min live

Most pipelines start life as a list of things the rep does: called, emailed, demo given, proposal sent. Every one of those can happen while the buyer does nothing. A stage that a rep can reach alone measures effort, and effort is not evidence that a deal will close. Rewrite each stage as an act by the buyer, and record where the proof lives.

LiveThree tests for a stage definition3 min▶

Who moved? If the rep can reach the stage without the buyer doing anything, it is an activity, not a stage. "Proposal sent" fails; "buyer asked for a price" passes.

Where is the proof? Each stage names one record a second person can check: a call note with the buyer's words, a trial log, an e-signature request. If the only proof is the rep's memory, the stage will drift with the rep's mood.

Can it go backwards? A trial that ends with "not this year" moves the deal back. A definition that only ever moves forward hides every stall. Analyst session 1 shows why most CRMs lose the backward moves unless somebody keeps the event log.

Self-studyWhat the CRM vendors do by default2 min▶

HubSpot's default deal pipeline, read on its knowledge base in October 2026, ships seven stages: Appointment scheduled, Qualified to buy, Presentation scheduled, Decision maker bought-in, Contract sent, Closed won and Closed lost. Two of the five open stages are things a rep can do alone (schedule, present). Salesforce's defaults are reported by third-party guides to follow a similar sales-process naming. Defaults are a starting point for a team that has not thought about it yet, not a definition of evidence; rename them to buyer acts before the first forecast relies on them. Re-verify the vendor pages before you teach from them: they change.

Real world

The demo that was a stage. Kettle's pipeline used to have "Demo given" as stage 3. Ben, the most energetic rep, gave tasting demos to anyone who would sit down, including receptionists with no budget. His stage-3 count was the highest on the team and his win rate the lowest. Renaming stage 3 to "Tasting trial: the office agreed to a station for two weeks" cut his stage-3 deals by half overnight and made the stage worth reading. Constructed Kettle & Co example; the pattern is common.

Part 2 · covers the four numbers

Four numbers carry every pipeline

5 min live

However many dashboards sit on top, a pipeline rests on four numbers: how many deals are in it, what share of them win, how big a won deal is, and how long a deal takes to close. Each has an honest definition and a flattering one, and the gap between them is where most pipeline arguments live.

LiveThe flattering version of each number3 min▶

Deals. Counting every contact as a deal makes the pipeline look full and the win rate look terrible. In Kettle's quarter 153 of 300 open deals sit at stage 1, Contacted; none of the 1,022 stage-1 deals in the previous quarter closed inside that quarter. Report stage 1 separately as leads.

Win rate. "Won this month divided by deals created this month" mixes two groups of deals. Win rate is the share of deals that entered together and have now been decided. Analyst session 2 shows how the period version swings by tens of points on the real rows.

Deal size. The average asked price includes deals that will be discounted or never signed. Use the median signed amount, and report the largest deal separately, because one whale can move a quarter (Analyst session 6 breaks the bench with exactly that).

Cycle time. On the real Olist rows, the median won deal closed 14 days after first contact; a quarter of them took 54.75 days or more, a tenth took 161.9 days or more, and the mean is 48.4 days. Any formula that uses "average cycle time" is using the 48.4.

Self-studyWhy the real rows are a Brazilian marketplace, not a tea company2 min▶

Public B2B pipeline data is rare: companies do not publish their CRM. Olist, a Brazilian marketplace, published 8,000 marketing-qualified leads from businesses that asked to sell through it (first contact June 2017 to May 2018) and the 842 that signed, with dates. That is a real B2B funnel with real timings. It has no lost deals and no stage history, so every stage, amount and rep on these pages is constructed and labelled. The same rows feed the funnel bench in Marketing, session 4 ↗ for budget and acquisition cost; here they are used for cycle time and forecasting.

Part 3 · covers the velocity identity

Velocity: the four numbers multiplied out

5 min live

Pipeline velocity is the four numbers in one line: deals times win rate times deal size, divided by cycle length. The answer is revenue per day the pipeline produces. It is an identity, not a forecast: it says what the four numbers imply if they hold. Its use is to show which lever moves revenue, and its trap is the denominator.

LiveWhat velocity is good for, and what it is not3 min▶

Good for: choosing a lever. Because it is a product, a 10 percent gain on any one of the three top terms is worth the same 10 percent of velocity. Cutting cycle length by 10 percent is worth a little more (one over 0.9 is 1.11). That makes a useful argument about where to spend effort, and it is arithmetic, not evidence.

Not good for: a forecast. Velocity assumes the four numbers hold, that every deal behaves like the typical one, and that the deals in the pipe today are like the ones that closed before. Analyst session 4 builds forecasts that test those assumptions instead of assuming them.

No single origin. The velocity formula circulates in sales-operations writing without one source to cite. Treat it as a convention whose arithmetic you can check, which is a better position than citing a name.

Self-studyWhy the median is still not the whole answer2 min▶

The median describes the typical won deal. Revenue, though, often sits in the slow tail: big offices take longer to sign. If your largest deals close slowest, a velocity built on the median can overstate how soon the money arrives. The honest version is two numbers, median and 75th percentile, and a sentence on which deals sit in the tail. Analyst session 3 draws the full distribution and the curve for deals still open.

Real world

The board slide that tripled overnight. Kettle's BD lead presented velocity using the CRM's "average days to close". A new analyst rebuilt it with the median and velocity jumped about 3.5-fold, with nothing in the business changed. The right response was neither number alone: both, labelled, plus the 75th percentile. Constructed Kettle & Co example using the real Olist median and mean.

Build-along

Rewrite your stages as buyer acts

★ 22 min · everyone works it through

No code, one page. Take your own pipeline's stage list, or Kettle's old one (Called, Demo given, Proposal sent, Negotiation). Five steps turn it into stages that are evidence. Do step 1 together, steps 2 to 4 in pairs, then read your step 5 velocity line aloud. Your stages will differ from Kettle's, which is the point.

Step 1 · List what you have. Write your current stage names in order, exactly as the CRM shows them. Mark each one R if the rep can reach it alone, B if the buyer has to do something.

★ Step 1 · today's stagesstage 1: ______________ R / B stage 2: ______________ R / B stage 3: ______________ R / B stage 4: ______________ R / B Kettle's old list: Called (R) · Demo given (R) · Proposal sent (R) · Negotiation (B)

Step 2 · Rewrite every R as a buyer act. Ask: what would the buyer have to do for this to mean something? "Demo given" becomes "the office agreed to a two-week trial station". Keep stage 1 as the one rep-only stage and call it what it is.

★ Step 2 · buyer acts1 Contacted (rep only; report as leads, not deals) 2 ______________ buyer act: ________________________________ 3 ______________ buyer act: ________________________________ 4 ______________ buyer act: ________________________________ 5 ______________ buyer act: ________________________________

Step 3 · Name the record. For each stage, the one record a second person could open to check it. If no such record exists today, write what would have to be logged.

★ Step 3 · the proofstage 2 proof: ____________________ (e.g. call note with the buyer's words) stage 3 proof: ____________________ (e.g. signed trial log) stage 4 proof: ____________________ (e.g. buyer's price request email) stage 5 proof: ____________________ (e.g. e-signature request sent to procurement)

Step 4 · Allow the backward move. For each stage, write what sends a deal back, and confirm somebody records the date it happened. Without dates you cannot rebuild last month's pipeline, which is the first thing Analyst session 1 needs.

★ Step 4 · backward movesa deal leaves stage 3 backwards when: ______________________ the date of that move is stored in: ______________________ Kettle example: trial ends "not this year" -> back to stage 2, logged with date

Step 5 · One velocity line, two cycle numbers. Fill in your four numbers with the honest definitions, and compute velocity twice: with the median cycle and with the mean. Read the two aloud and say which one your dashboard shows today.

★ Step 5 · velocity, twicedeals (stage 2+): ____ win rate (by entry period): ____ median deal size: ____ velocity, median cycle (__ days): ____ per day velocity, mean cycle (__ days): ____ per day Kettle example (constructed counts, real Olist cycle): 40 x 25% x 480,000 yen / 14 days = 342,857 a day ; / 48.4 days = 99,174 a day

Keep the sheet. The stage list is page one of the forecast contract in Business session 3. If a stage has no record, the analyst cannot measure it, and a forecast built on it inherits the rep's mood.

30 min

  2. Find your cycle-time definition. Ask whoever owns the CRM report whether "average days to close" is a mean or a median, and from which date to which.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Business session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Which of these is a pipeline stage that counts as evidence?

A stage the rep can reach alone measures effort. A buyer act with a record a second person can check is evidence.

2 · On the real Olist rows the median won deal took 14 days and the mean 48.4. What does that do to a velocity formula that uses "average cycle time"?

The few deals that took months pull the mean up. Report which cycle number you used, and show the 75th percentile with it.

3 · What is pipeline velocity?

It says what the four numbers imply if they hold. It has no single origin, and it assumes away everything a forecast has to test.
